# Cable Standards: EIA and TIA

While the IEEE 802.3 standards define the protocols for how data is transmitted across a network, they do not dictate the physical manufacturing specifications of the copper cables themselves. These physical standards—which define requirements such as wire gauge, twist rate, insulation materials, and electromagnetic interference tolerance—are developed by the **Electronic Industries Alliance (EIA)** and the **Telecommunications Industry Association (TIA)**.

These organizations establish **Category** (Cat) ratings to classify copper cabling based on its electrical performance. Higher category ratings denote stricter manufacturing requirements, which allow the cable to handle higher signal frequencies with less signal degradation. Selecting the correct cable category is essential for ensuring that the physical medium can reliably support the data transmission speeds required by the IEEE protocols. 

The following table outlines the relationship between common cable categories, their typical performance, and the speeds they are designed to support in a network environment:

| Speed | Informal Name | Cable Name |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 10 Mbps | Ethernet | **Cat3** |
| 100 Mbps | Fast Ethernet | **Cat5** |
| 1 Gbps | Gigabit Ethernet | **Cat5e** |
| 10 Gbps | 10 Gigabit Ethernet | **Cat6a** |
