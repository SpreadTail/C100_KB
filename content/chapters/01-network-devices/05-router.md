# Routers

While a switch connects devices within a single network, a router is required to connect different networks to one another. The primary role of a router is to provide connectivity between a Local Area Network (LAN) and external networks, such as the Internet or other remote LANs. It acts as the gateway for data entering or leaving the local environment. By analyzing the destination of incoming and outgoing data, the router determines the most efficient path for information to travel, ensuring that data packets move correctly across different network segments.

> It is important to distinguish between specialized networking equipment and the standard equipment found in most households. A typical home router is a multifunctional device that combines the roles of several different network components into a single unit. Although it is commonly referred to simply as a "router", it functions as a combination of a router, a network switch, and a wireless access point. In many configurations, it also incorporates basic firewall security. By bundling these roles into one device, it provides a practical, all-in-one solution that allows multiple devices in a home or small office to connect to each other and to the Internet simultaneously.
