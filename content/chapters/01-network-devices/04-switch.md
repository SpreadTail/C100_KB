# Switches

The primary function of a switch is to connect multiple devices together within a Local Area Network (LAN). Acting as the central intersection for a network segment, the switch facilitates the physical and logical interconnection of various hardware components, ensuring they operate as a unified system.

Devices connected to a switch are able to communicate with each other directly through the device. When one node sends data, the switch manages the traffic, directing the information specifically to the intended destination node rather than broadcasting it to every device on the network. This process creates a private, efficient pathway for communication between the connected end hosts.

> To accommodate numerous devices, switches are designed with high-density connectivity. A standard switch typically features multiple physical ports(commonly ranging from 24 to 48) allowing a large number of end hosts, such as computers, printers, and servers, to be plugged in simultaneously. By providing these connection points, the switch acts as the primary hardware infrastructure that allows all local devices to interact and share resources within the same network.
